## Stage 1 — subtractive spine (Vital)

### Ex 1 — waveform vocabulary · 2026-08-14

- Waveform = a recipe for how loud each harmonic is. Sine's has one ingredient.
- Note-on transient is its own event, separate from steady state — add attack to compare waveforms fairly. **Init attack stays 0**; 20 ms would fight Ex 3's pluck and Ex 5's sub. Anti-click, if ever wanted: 1–3 ms.
- Sine vs triangle = **vowel colour** (front/back articulation). Vowels are about which harmonics are emphasised, so it's the mechanism, not a metaphor.
- Saw = nearly everything (brass, bowed strings, sax, oboe, flute); square = **the clarinet, specifically**. Driver is bore shape, not instrument family — clarinet overblows at the twelfth because it has no ×2.
- Saw/square differ in *which* harmonics exist (no evens); sine/triangle only in *how much* upper content. First is the sharper distinction.
- Saw and square **don't** converge at C5 — their difference sits at ×2, too low to leave hearing. Ex 1 step 4 amended accordingly.
- Sawtooth's shape = bow stick-slip: grip, drag, snap. Same physics as a creaking hinge.
- Octave-to-octave character: **monotonic = the sound, non-monotonic = the chain** (room, headphones, ears).
